Maulana Bisyir Azhari

I am a PhD Candidate in Electrical Engineering at Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology (KAIST), where I work at the Unmanned System Research Group (USRG) under the supervision of Prof. David Hyunchul Shim. My research focuses on visual and inertial state estimation for autonomous drones — spanning learning-based visual odometry/SLAM, high-speed state estimation for autonomous drone racing, and real-world autonomous systems for maritime security and exploration. I have defended my PhD thesis, with title: Specialized and Generalized Visual and Inertial State Estimation: From Autonomous Drone Racing to Diverse Environments.

DINO-VO (IEEE RA-L 2025)

Robust camera-based localization that leverages learned features and visual foundation models, enabling GPS-free and LiDAR-free flight in challenging environments such as dark tunnels. My latest work, LiΣ-SLAM, is able to be deployed on Orin NX computer in real-time. My previous work, DINO-VO, integrated the robust, generalizable features of a visual foundation model in a feature-based visual odometry pipeline.
Related: LiΣ-SLAM (in preparation), DINO-VO (IEEE RA-L 2025, presented at IEEE ICRA 2026).

Team KAIST at the 2026 A2RL Drone Championship AI Speed Race

A2RL Autonomous Drone Racing Season 1 (April, 2025)

High-speed visual-inertial and learning-based inertial state estimation using only a single monocular RGB camera and an IMU, developed for the A2RL Autonomous Drone Championship. My work spans a tightly-coupled filter-based monocular VIO with graph-based evaluation, drift correction combined with perception-aware planning, and VeloBins, which learns velocity and its uncertainty directly from inertial data.
Related: Drift-Corrected VIO (JFR 2026), ADR-VINS (IEEE IROS 2026), VeloBins (under revision at IEEE RA-L).

SPIBOT: A Drone-Tethered Mobile Gripper for Aerial Grasping (ICRA 2025)

UAV-USV Relative Localization at MBZIRC

A collaborative UAV–USV system to detect illegal vessels and retrieve stolen objects in a GPS-denied marine environment — 2nd place at MBZIRC 2024. The system relies on AprilTag-based and optical-flow localization, I also developed the mission planner, control, and real-time object detection and tracking.
Related: SPIBOT (IEEE ICRA 2025).

Autonomous Drone Exploration

Autonomous Exploration in Unknown Environments (Army Tiger DroneBot Challenge)

Autonomous Exploration with AI-Powered Artifact Detection (ADD Battlefield Drone)

Fully autonomous indoor reconnaissance with LiDAR-inertial odometry, exploration, and onboard object detection — 1st place at the Army Tiger DroneBot Challenge and Top 2 at the ADD Battlefield Drone competition. The drones enter unknown, GPS-denied buildings, explore them, locate hidden targets in real time, and return to the start using only onboard computation.
Related: Adaptive LIO with Practical UAV Exploration (JFR 2024).
